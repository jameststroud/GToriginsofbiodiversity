In-class graduate synthesis presentation

To be assigned · About the presentations

Species are the currency of biodiversity, yet biologists still disagree about what a species actually is. Ernst Mayr defined species by reproductive isolation, coining the term “biological species concept,” while other concepts emphasize ecology, morphology, or ancestry, revealing that the needs of taxonomy and of evolutionary biology do not always align. Speciation is best viewed as a continuum, from populations that interbreed freely to lineages that never will. Along it, reproductive isolation accumulates through barriers that act before or after fertilization (prezygotic and postzygotic), arising either from intrinsic genetic incompatibilities or from the external environment. However, geography is also of fundamental importance: allopatric speciation (divergence in geographic isolation) has long been treated as the default, due to ongoing debates on the degree to which sympatric speciation may occur. But, how often is speciation the result of divergent natural selection between environments, rather than separation alone, that drives two populations apart?
