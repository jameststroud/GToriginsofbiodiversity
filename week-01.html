In-class graduate synthesis presentation

To be assigned · About the presentations

In many different ways, life is not spread evenly across the planet. Before Darwin, most naturalists saw that variety as fixed (Linnaeus) or as directed toward ever greater complexity (Lamarck), rather than as the product of an undirected process. Darwin (1859) famously supplied that process: evolution by natural selection. Yet Darwin lacked a working theory of inheritance, which was a critical piece of information to provide a complete theory. In the early to mid-20th century, with the rise of genetics, the architects of the Modern Synthesis closed this gap, fully formalizing a “neo-Darwinian” theory of evolution. A century after Darwin, G. Evelyn Hutchinson (1959) asked the question this course is built around: why are there so many kinds of animals? Whether the Modern Synthesis, built around genes and natural selection, is enough to answer it remains an active debate, and one we will return to all semester.
