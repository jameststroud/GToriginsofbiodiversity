In-class graduate synthesis presentation

To be assigned · About the presentations

Divergence in isolation (i.e., allopatry) is only half the story. When diverged populations meet again in space, secondary contact becomes the moment of truth. Perhaps, the most exciting situation any evolutionary biologist can witness. These two “incipient species” (i.e., populations) may hybridize and fuse back into one species, one may exclude the other through competition or reproductive interference, or both may persist side by side. But, importantly, interactions during secondary contact can themselves drive evolution. Brown and Wilson (1956) described character displacement, in which coexisting species diverge where they overlap, and Dobzhansky championed reinforcement, in which selection against unfit hybrids completes reproductive isolation. However, one curiosity revealed in contemporary evolutionary biology is that even species that remain distinct may occasionally exchange genes through introgression. Increasingly, we suspect that the patterns we see in nature reflect species sorting as much as evolutionary change: contact may determine which species pairs persist at all, not just how they evolve once they meet.
