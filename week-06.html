In-class graduate synthesis presentation

To be assigned · About the presentations

Why do some lineages diversify into dozens of ecologically distinct species while their close relatives do not? The answer canonized by George Gaylord Simpson, and made the centerpiece of an ecological theory of adaptive radiation by Dolph Schluter, is ecological opportunity: access to evolutionarily-accessible resources (i.e., those unused, or underused, by competitors). Simpson regarded access to “ecological opportunity” occurring in various scenarios: the colonization of a new depauperate region (e.g., a newly-formed island), surviving a mass extinction, or evolving a “key innovation” that allowed a lineage to use a new part of the environment or resource spectrum. But opportunity does not always lead to radiation, and not every radiation is adaptive. Why?
