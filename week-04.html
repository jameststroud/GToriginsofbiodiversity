In-class graduate synthesis presentation

To be assigned · About the presentations

Why do some species occur together while others never do? Stemming from Hutchinson's concept of an ecological “niche”, and MacArthur's (1958) subsequent example of warblers foraging in different parts of the same spruce trees, suggested that competition sorts species into communities by their differences. Yet patterns such as forbidden pairs (ecologically similar species that appear never to co-occur) sparked a heated debate through the late 1970s and early 1980s over whether chance alone could produce them, establishing null models as a standard tool of community ecology (the “null model wars”!) Hubbell's neutral theory pushed the question further: how much of community structure can we explain if species are treated as ecologically equivalent? Most recently, greater recognition has been given to the role of history, as the order in which species arrive (priority effects) may shape a community as much as environmental filtering does, helping explain why sites with similar conditions can differ so much in which species they hold (beta diversity).
