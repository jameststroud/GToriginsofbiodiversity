In-class graduate synthesis presentation

To be assigned · About the presentations

Darwin thought natural selection far too slow to watch: “We see nothing of these slow changes in progress, until the hand of time has marked the long lapse of ages.” Contemporary evolutionary biology has surprisingly suggested Darwin was wrong. From the 1970s onward, field biologists such as Peter and Rosemary Grant documented intense selection and adaptive change on observable timescales. Pivotal to the development of these ideas, Lande and Arnold (1983) provided a statistical framework for measuring the form and strength of selection directly in the wild. However, this revelation that strong natural selection can be detected in wild populations leaves an enduring puzzle: if selection in the wild can be strong, and many traits are heritable, why do many (most?) populations change far less than these measurements predict? Resolving this “paradox of stasis”, and understanding where phenotypic plasticity fits into it, is one of the central challenges in evolutionary biology today.
