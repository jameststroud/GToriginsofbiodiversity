In-class graduate synthesis presentation

To be assigned · About the presentations

If evolution can happen on ecological timescales, can evolution in turn change ecology? Hutchinson (1965) pictured an ecological theater hosting an evolutionary play, an image long read as ecology setting the stage on which evolution slowly unfolds. The discovery of contemporary evolution blurred that division, raising the possibility of eco-evolutionary feedbacks, in which ecological change drives evolution and evolution in turn reshapes populations, communities, and ecosystems. Guppies in the streams of Trinidad, where predation differs sharply above and below waterfalls, have become a flagship test of the idea, however even in this system it is unclear to what extent such “eco-evolutionary dynamics” occur at a meaningful level. With human activity now imposing strong selection on many species, the central question is practical as well as theoretical: when is evolutionary change large enough, and fast enough, to matter ecologically, and can we predict it?
