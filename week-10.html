In-class graduate synthesis presentation

To be assigned · About the presentations

Why do birds sing, lizards perform push-up displays, and gazelles stot (leap stiff-legged in front of predators)? Each question has four correct answers, which Niko Tinbergen famously separated into mechanism, development, function, and evolutionary history, arguing that a full account of behavior needs all four. Behavior is often the most flexible part of the phenotype, allowing animals to respond to new conditions and, in doing so, to alter the selection they experience. Sociality poses a sharper puzzle, one that troubled Darwin: how can selection favor behavior that helps others at a cost to the actor? W. D. Hamilton’s answer, inclusive fitness and kin selection, transformed the study of social behavior. Yet Gould and Lewontin’s (1979) warning against assuming that every trait is an adaptation still hangs over adaptive explanations of behavior.
