In-class graduate synthesis presentation

To be assigned · About the presentations

Species are not independent data points. Because relatives share traits through common ancestry, Felsenstein (1985) showed that any comparison across species must account for phylogeny, turning comparative biology into a phylogenetic discipline. Phylogenies now let us reconstruct how traits evolved, measure phylogenetic diversity and signal, ask whether communities are phylogenetically clustered or overdispersed (made up of closer or more distant relatives than expected by chance), and estimate rates of speciation and extinction from the shape of the tree. However, each of these tasks come with assumptions and caveats, which are important to be aware of and discuss.
